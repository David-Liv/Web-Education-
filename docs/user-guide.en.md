# HOKSE User Guide

v1.2 · 4 November 2026 · Find your direction and make progress at your own pace.

Website: [Open HOKSE](https://web-education.onrender.com). Sign in with your invited account to begin. No software installation is needed.

## 1. Getting started

1. Choose your subjects and enter your current and target grades.
2. Set the time you can spend studying each day. You can choose university goals later.
3. Pick a unit in My Learning Flow and start with an explanation or a short exercise.
4. Preview your Study Calendar and add the plan when it suits you.

Use school estimates or mock exams as a starting point for whole-subject grades. Short exercises identify topic weaknesses; they do not automatically become a whole-subject DSE grade.

## 2. My Learning Flow

Mathematics, Chinese and English offer unit explanations, exercises and feedback. Choose a subject and a starting question count, then pick what you need:

- Fixed quick practice: try a method and revisit it at your pace.
- AI quick exercises: generate short supporting questions.
- Independent quizzes: answer on your own before reviewing feedback and next steps.
- Guided reading and explanations: bring an article or question into a conversation.
- Writing suggestions: improve content, structure and expression step by step.

Use hints when needed. After submitting, review each answer. If something seems wrong, select “There is a problem with this feedback”. AI feedback is study guidance; discuss uncertain points with a teacher or tutor.

## 3. AI Study Room

Each conversation keeps a learning topic together. Rename it, continue asking questions, stop a reply or save a learning summary.

Replies can include tables, formulas, charts, function plots, basic geometry, concept maps and simple circuit schematics. Enlarge a diagram, download SVG/PNG, or open its supporting text or data where available. These are learning references; circuit diagrams are not simulations.

Case-by-case explanations can use relationship diagrams and comparison tables. Chat does not currently offer draggable sliders or interactive records with live calculations. To change example values, continue in the same conversation. Quick-practice answers can still contain mistakes; if an answer disagrees with its explanation, ask the AI to recalculate.

Three suggested next steps appear after each completed reply. Click one to fill the input box, edit it if you wish, then press Enter to continue in the same conversation. Nothing is sent by clicking a suggestion. If it replaces an existing draft, use “Restore your draft” to get that draft back.

Quick practice stays in the chat. Try answering below, or expand “Give me a hint” and “Show answer and explanation” when needed. These exercises support learning and do not count as independent quiz scores.

Suggestions, answer reveals, enlarging and downloads use no extra credits. Sending a new question is a new AI operation at the displayed model price. A reply may still provide useful text when a visual cannot be displayed.

Include the question, your own thinking and where you got stuck. With an image or recording, also describe the problem in text. Attachments currently support keeping and viewing material; attaching a file does not mean AI has read everything in it.

Listening and speaking features labelled “Demo” are for trying the experience, not formal listening or speaking assessment.

## 4. Daily Review and mistakes

Daily Review brings recent learning and due reviews together. Recall key points, try a short quiz, add notes or revisit a conversation summary.

The Mistake Notebook keeps answers and possible error causes. Find the step that went wrong, then use a different question to check understanding. Reading a solution, using hints or repeating the same question helps practice but does not by itself establish secure mastery.

## 5. Study Calendar and checklist

The calendar suggests activities around your available time and learning direction. Preview before adding them. You can then reschedule, adjust time slots or keep activities in the checklist.

- Lock activities you want to keep in place.
- Move an activity back to the checklist when the day is too busy.
- Set a rest day and return when it suits you.
- Daily Review is already included in your time budget.

Check the source and status shown for exam dates. Use officially announced timetables for exam preparation.

## 6. University goals

Explore programmes by institution, qualification, study area or keyword. Sort by admission-score index or competition ratio. Choose one main goal and up to two backups, then compare their requirements and features side by side.

Details show interview arrangements, recorded places, subject weightings, entry requirements and historical scores. Upper quartile, median and lower quartile are displayed separately; unavailable data stays unavailable.

After entering grades, you can see:

- How far your current and target grades each fall below or above the reference benchmark.
- Current or target reference lines on the historical chart.
- Which subject improvements help the main goal and which backups also benefit.

Figures are approximate gaps: platform reference scenarios, not admission probabilities or guarantees. Borrowed programme data is labelled with its benchmark and sources. QS rankings only indicate a possible upward or downward bias; they do not add or subtract points. Open “View scoring method and reference basis” to see the subjects and assumptions used.

Study plans focus on the main goal. After changing it, preview a new plan before deciding whether to apply it.

## 7. Overview and reports

The overview helps you revisit activities, tasks and topic progress. The Two-week Review brings together your recent records so you can adjust your next steps.

With limited records, start by looking at the days you have. Time recorded and tasks completed are not the same as learning outcomes. Notice whether you can explain ideas independently and handle new questions.

## 8. Resources and Maths By Topic

Search, filter and bookmark learning websites in Resources. Maths By Topic helps you find questions by topic, year and source, then visit the original website.

Source difficulty labels and target levels help you find material; they are not official per-question DSE grades. External content, fees and terms are set by the source website.

## 9. Personal settings and AI credits

Adjust subjects, grades, language and available time in Personal Settings. Plan & Billing shows available credits and lets you choose separate default models for quizzes, conversations and assessment. Each model displays its credit price per operation.

AI operations reserve credits first and charge when a usable result is available. Failures and cancellation release the reservation. Fixed-question generation and rule-only marking use no AI credits. If credits run out, you can still keep notes, do fixed practice and arrange your calendar.

The current checkout is a demonstration. Choosing a payment channel does not charge real money. The page shows your current allowance and plan arrangements.

## 10. Common questions

- AI cannot reply: try again later, or start with lessons, notes and fixed exercises.
- The plan does not fit: shorten an activity, reschedule it or keep it in the checklist.
- Feedback or information seems wrong: note the programme or question and tell the organiser what needs attention.
- The site takes time to open: allow it to load before continuing.

Use your own account and sign out before leaving a shared computer. For troubleshooting, administrators can view AI usage records and export response text. Avoid unnecessary personal secrets in your questions.
