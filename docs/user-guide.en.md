# HOKSE User Guide

v1.7 · 11 November 2026 · Find your direction and make progress at your own pace.

Website: [Open HOKSE](https://web-education.onrender.com). Sign in with your invited account to begin. No software installation is needed.

On desktop, use the button at the top of the sidebar to collapse navigation to icons. Hover over an icon or focus it with the keyboard to see its name. Your choice is remembered in this browser. On mobile, open navigation with the menu button.

## 1. Getting started

1. Choose your subjects and enter your current and target grades.
2. Set the time you can spend studying each day. You can choose university goals later.
3. Pick a unit in My Learning Flow and start with an explanation or a short exercise.
4. Preview your Study Calendar and add the plan when it suits you.

Use school estimates or mock exams as a starting point for whole-subject grades. Short exercises identify topic weaknesses; they do not automatically become a whole-subject DSE grade.

## 2. My Learning Flow

Mathematics, Chinese and English offer unit explanations, exercises and feedback. Choose a subject and a starting question count, then pick what you need:

- Fixed quick practice: try a method and revisit it at your pace.
- AI quick exercises: generate short supporting questions.
- Independent quizzes: answer on your own before reviewing feedback and next steps.
- Guided reading and explanations: bring an article or question into a conversation.
- Writing suggestions: improve content, structure and expression step by step.

Use hints when needed. After submitting, review each answer. If something seems wrong, select “There is a problem with this feedback”. AI feedback is study guidance; discuss uncertain points with a teacher or tutor.

### Focus on one skill

In Mathematics, use “Focus on one skill, then try something new”. Choose standard form and coefficients, factorisation, the quadratic formula, the discriminant, parameter conditions, or contextual modelling. Read the short explanation and try one to five questions, with three as the default. Review a prerequisite first when needed.

Enter numbers, fractions or expressions such as (5+sqrt(13))/2. For modelling, also enter the equation you formed. A final number alone does not establish the method. An expression that cannot be reliably checked is saved for further reference checking.

“Download practice PDF” uses the same questions and numbers. Print it, then follow its link back to enter your answers. Repeat exports use no AI credits.

Use “Question this judgement” to explain what should be checked. Each answer can be reviewed automatically once, at no credit cost to you. The result may correct or uphold the judgement, or leave it uncertain and excluded. Your original answer is retained; reviewing it does not create another attempt.

## 3. AI Study Room

Choose a teaching method when starting a conversation. David offers guided reading, gradual hints and step-by-step problem solving. Matt builds short PDF lessons around your goal, followed by practice and discussion. Matt’s first complete topic is quadratic equations in compulsory Mathematics; the selector shows which topics are available.

Each conversation keeps its tutor. “Change tutor” opens a new conversation while keeping the original. Relevant learning notes can carry across. Teaching settings do not change your grade targets or calendar. In “Learning settings”, add your goal, constraints and preferences, and revisit lesson references and learning notes. Confirm notes that match your understanding to help your tutor teach what comes next.

Matt provides a downloadable PDF for a new lesson. Ordinary follow-up questions stay in the chat. Choose “Next PDF lesson”, check the draft and send it when you want to continue. After reading, choose “Discuss this lesson” and include its lesson number, version and question number, such as “Lesson 1, v1, Q2”, followed by your answer or question. Lessons provide questions first; ask for hints or a worked answer whenever you need them.

“Revise content” fills a draft for you to edit and send. Revisions create a new version; earlier versions remain available. Opening and repeat downloads use no credits. A new lesson or content revision costs one operation at your conversation model price, including automatic exercise checks. An undelivered lesson is not charged. For file layout problems, use the free rebuild option under “Lesson details” to reformat the saved content. Downloading or reading a lesson does not automatically establish formal mastery.

New exercises and worked answers in chat are checked before appearing. General explanations may appear first, with practice added to the same reply afterward. For requests that only ask for questions or solutions, wait for the complete reply. These checks help reduce errors; replies remain AI learning references.

Started work continues when you switch conversations or leave the page. Return to see saved results; use Stop to cancel explicitly. If a reply could not be completed, Retry continues the same saved request once without an additional charge or rewriting an explanation already delivered. After another failure, send a new request. There are no automatic retry loops.

Each conversation keeps a learning topic together. Rename it, continue asking questions, stop a reply or save a learning summary.

Replies can include tables, formulas, charts, function plots, basic geometry, concept maps and simple circuit schematics. Enlarge a diagram, download SVG/PNG, or open its supporting text or data where available. These are learning references; circuit diagrams are not simulations.

Case-by-case explanations can use relationship diagrams and comparison tables. Chat does not currently offer draggable sliders or interactive records with live calculations. To change example values, continue in the same conversation. Quick-practice answers can still contain mistakes; if an answer disagrees with its explanation, ask the AI to recalculate.

Suggested next steps appear when your tutor chooses to include them. Click one to fill the input box, edit it if you wish, then press Enter to continue in the same conversation. Nothing is sent by clicking a suggestion. If it replaces an existing draft, use “Restore your draft” to get that draft back.

When your tutor uses chat exercises, quick practice stays in the chat. Try answering below, or expand “Give me a hint” and “Show answer and explanation” when needed. These exercises support learning and do not count as independent quiz scores.

Suggestions, answer reveals, enlarging and downloads use no extra credits. Sending a new question is a new AI operation at the conversation model price shown in Plan & Billing. Automatic review has no additional student charge. No usable delivery means no charge; a delivered explanation retains its original charge. A previously refunded request is charged its original operation once if its retry succeeds. A reply may still provide useful text when a visual cannot be displayed.

Include the question, your own thinking and where you got stuck. With an image or recording, also describe the problem in text. Attachments currently support keeping and viewing material; attaching a file does not mean AI has read everything in it.

Listening and speaking features labelled “Demo” are for trying the experience, not formal listening or speaking assessment.

Choose “Answer this question” under a chat exercise, or the answer button for a PDF question, then send your answer directly. A small label identifies the question. Close it to return to ordinary discussion. You can also specify the lesson, version and question number. Select the actual question if its identity is unclear. Ordinary questions are not scored as answers. Tutors share your skill history while retaining the tutor and document version used for each attempt.

## 4. Daily Review and mistakes

Daily Review brings recent learning and due reviews together. Recall key points, try a short quiz, add notes or revisit a conversation summary.

After targeted practice, the skill appears under “For your next practice”. Activities for the same skill and learning date are merged. Start a retest with one or two new questions, or practise early. Moving to the next calendar date alone does not establish stable understanding: at least 24 hours, different question families and independent performance are considered together. Calendar changes still require a preview; items that do not fit remain unscheduled.

The Mistake Notebook keeps answers and possible error causes. “Practice answers to revisit” also shows these practice answers, feedback and sources, with a link to try new questions. Find the step that went wrong, then use a different question to check understanding. Reading a solution, using hints or repeating the same question helps practice but does not by itself establish secure mastery.

## 5. Study Calendar and checklist

The calendar suggests activities around your available time and learning direction. Preview before adding them. You can then reschedule, adjust time slots or keep activities in the checklist.

Drag a pending task on desktop, or touch and hold before moving it on a phone or tablet. Ordinary swipes still scroll. Day/week views snap to 5-minute intervals; month view changes the date only. Duration stays unchanged. Preview the destination while dragging and release to save. A “Schedule updated · Undo” reminder lasts **3 seconds** and can undo the most recent move. Click a task to edit its details.

Completed tasks, locked tasks and exams cannot be dragged. Overlaps, over-budget days, rest days and moves past midnight are rejected with a specific reason. Short tasks use a single-line title; hover or open the details to read the full text.

- Lock activities you want to keep in place.
- Move an activity back to the checklist when the day is too busy.
- Set a rest day and return when it suits you.
- Daily Review is already included in your time budget.

Check the source and status shown for exam dates. Use officially announced timetables for exam preparation.

## 6. University goals

Explore programmes by institution, qualification, study area or keyword. Sort by admission-score index or competition ratio. Choose one main goal and up to two backups, then compare their requirements and features side by side.

Details show interview arrangements, recorded places, subject weightings, entry requirements and historical scores. Upper quartile, median and lower quartile are displayed separately; unavailable data stays unavailable.

After entering grades, you can see:

- How far your current and target grades each fall below or above the reference benchmark.
- Current or target reference lines on the historical chart.
- Which subject improvements help the main goal and which backups also benefit.

Figures are approximate gaps: platform reference scenarios, not admission probabilities or guarantees. Borrowed programme data is labelled with its benchmark and sources. QS rankings only indicate a possible upward or downward bias; they do not add or subtract points. Open “View scoring method and reference basis” to see the subjects and assumptions used.

Study plans focus on the main goal. After changing it, preview a new plan before deciding whether to apply it.

## 7. Overview and reports

The overview helps you revisit activities, tasks and topic progress. The Two-week Review brings together your recent records so you can adjust your next steps.

With limited records, start by looking at the days you have. Time recorded and tasks completed are not the same as learning outcomes. Notice whether you can explain ideas independently and handle new questions.

## 8. Resources and Maths By Topic

Search, filter and bookmark learning websites in Resources. Maths By Topic helps you find questions by topic, year and source, then visit the original website.

Source difficulty labels and target levels help you find material; they are not official per-question DSE grades. External content, fees and terms are set by the source website.

## 9. Personal settings and AI credits

Adjust subjects, grades, language and available time in Personal Settings. Plan & Billing shows available credits and lets you choose separate default models for quizzes, conversations and assessment. Each model displays its credit price per operation.

AI operations reserve credits first and charge when a usable result is available. Failure or cancellation releases the reservation if nothing usable was delivered. An explanation already delivered retains its original charge. Fixed-question generation and rule-only marking use no AI credits. If credits run out, you can still keep notes, do fixed practice and arrange your calendar.

The current checkout is a demonstration. Choosing a payment channel does not charge real money. The page shows your current allowance and plan arrangements.

## 10. Common questions

- AI cannot reply: try again later, or start with lessons, notes and fixed exercises.
- The plan does not fit: shorten an activity, reschedule it or keep it in the checklist.
- Feedback or information seems wrong: note the programme or question and tell the organiser what needs attention.
- The site takes time to open: allow it to load before continuing.

Use your own account and sign out before leaving a shared computer. For troubleshooting, administrators can view AI usage records and export response text. Avoid unnecessary personal secrets in your questions.
